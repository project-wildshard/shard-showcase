# Driftwood Isle

A single hand-made MMO zone, played alone: open ocean and cool islands
of every shape and size, with islets, reefs and sea stacks, under a
huge ringed planet. Think The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker and
Assassin's Creed IV: Black Flag, with Moana and Sea of Thieves. You
arrive by sailboat with nothing but a driftwood sword. It should feel
sunny, cute and adventurous, a little spooky at night, like the best
day of summer from the first step to the last.

## Shape

It is one zone of a bigger MMO world, joined to its neighbours by
roads, so it fits inside one 500 × 500 × 500 m cube and never grows.
Four edge islands sit at the middle of the cube's four sides, with
the sea's surface halfway up, each with a road running off the edge to
the rest of the world; nothing beyond needs to exist yet. All three
mini dungeons sit inside the cube too.

- **Use the whole cube, every corner of it.** Ocean and islands of
  every shape and size fill the full 500 × 500 m footprint: islets,
  reefs, sea stacks and sandbars between them. It fills the full
  height too, from the deep sea floor at the cube's bottom to the
  highest island peak at its top. Open sea runs between them, but it
  is never empty.
- **Two worlds, equal in size:** there is just as much under the sea
  as above it. Below the water lies a completely dry, hidden world you
  can enter, as large as the surface world, as dense, varied and worth
  exploring, with its own places, people, quests, secrets and set
  pieces.
- **Shallow and deep:** warm, clear shallows over sand and reef, and
  deep blue water that drops away beneath you into the dark.
- **Every way across:** your own sailboat, swimming and diving,
  sandbars bared at low tide and a harpoon that hauls you along, with
  ziplines, rope bridges and cliff dives as extras.
- **One open zone**, no fixed sides: go anywhere, see anything you can
  reach.
- **Filled with places**, above and below the sea, each with its own
  look, life and danger.
- **Five or more epic set pieces**: moments a player remembers and
  wants to show someone.
- Each set piece must work as a ten-second clip, filmed on a phone in
  portrait, that makes someone ask "what game is that?"

## In the zone

To get a feeling for what to build, here are some ideas for the zone
that you can start with:

- **Your own small sailboat, sailed anywhere.** A real vehicle you
  steer yourself: catch the wind, crest the swells, slide into the
  shallows, beach it on any shore and dive off the bow. Whistle and it
  sails to you. Fit it out as you level: a bigger sail, a lantern, a
  diving bell.
- **One pirate ship roams the ocean.** Sail alongside, jump and climb
  aboard, and fight the pirates on her decks.
- **The open sea is a show:** wind you can see on the water, big
  rolling swells, dolphins and flying fish racing the bow, and storms
  you watch come in from far off, with waterspouts and lightning.
- **An undersea world as big as the one above.** At first you can only
  hold your breath for short dives. A quest wins you an underwater
  breather, and then the whole sea floor opens up, as deep as it goes.
- Dry places under the sea, for example air pockets, domed halls,
  caves, sunken structures and more.
- Above the sea: islands of every kind, from a pirate port and a
  lighthouse rock to a smoking volcano, jungle isles, sea-stack
  villages and sandbars that come and go.
- **The planet moves the sea.** As it swings low the tide drains away,
  baring the reef, the wreck and paths you could never walk; as it
  climbs again the sea pours back in, fast.
- Optional extras: ziplines, rope bridges, cliffs and cliff dives.
- Secrets, vistas, caches and collectibles all over, above and below
  the sea.
- Levels 1 to 20, levelled by fighting.
- Three mini dungeons: a wreck on the rocks, walked dry at low tide
  and swum when the sea pours back in; a whirlpool far out at sea,
  which you sail your own boat into and ride down the spiral to a
  fortress at the bottom, walled in by spinning water; and a sunken
  palace in the deepest trench, swum through entirely under water by
  the glow of the things that live there.

## Features

- Underwater exploration.
- A sailboat that you can drive.
- Swimming and diving.
- Underwater combat.
- A zone under the sea: a completely dry, hidden world under the
  water that you can enter, as large as the surface world.

## Main quest

An epic quest line in many steps that takes you across the whole
zone, above and below the sea, and ends in an epic final boss fight.
The story, the steps and the boss are yours to design.

## Look

- Faceted, flat-shaded low-poly with toon lighting: coloured shadows
  and a rim of light on every edge. Never photoreal, never grey.
- Turquoise shallows, white foam, lush greens, warm sand, chunky
  faceted clouds, and the ringed planet always hanging over the sea.
- A long bright day and a short moonlit night, blue, never black.
- The Wind Waker as the inspiration.

## Weapons

- Melee: the driftwood sword you wash up with, which grows with you,
  level by level, into a great living blade, barnacled and edged with
  coral, whose full swing throws a breaking wave down the line; a
  stormglass trident that charges with every hit, then lets it out in
  one thrust that arcs lightning through every foe in the water; and
  more.
- Ranged: a harpoon gun on a long rope that reels a foe in, or you to
  it; a twin-barrel flintlock, one barrel for the air and one that
  fires a spinning bolt straight through the water; a lighthouse lens
  on a brass stock that gathers sunlight into a beam that burns hotter
  the longer you hold it; and more.
- The harpoon works under water too, and it moves you: fire it into a
  cliff, a mast or a big fish and get hauled along.

## Enemies

- Fill the islands, the sea and the world below with enemies as you
  see fit: on land, at sea and under the water.
- Like an MMO, they come in level bands, each with its own part of
  the world: 1–3, 4–6, 7–9, 10–12, 13–15 and 16–18.
- Level 20 is the endgame: bosses and endgame content worth coming
  back for.
- Many enemies, each with a rare variant. Elites are scattered around.
- A final boss at the end of the main quest, at level 20.

## Quests

- Side quests.
- Happenings.
- A bounty notice at the camp, with a joke title for every first kill.
