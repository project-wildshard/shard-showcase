# Let Rook's shoulder camera follow the first cut, then give it back

Use the pinned Fable build on loopback. Run `node demos/mite-yard/jack-warning/native-corner.mjs FABLE_CHECKOUT DEPENDENCY_PROJECT 3 inside left shoulder-live` and repeat with `shoulder-look`. Pass both replay output directories to `corner-shoulder-live-proof.mjs`. This uses the same existing crate and trusted touch sword tap as the original replay.

The candidate eases toward 35 cm right and 45 cm up after the native light attack targets Jack, then returns to stock. It keeps Jack's core anchored, adjusts only the rendered lens, restores it before the next update, and checks both the local displacement from the stock position and the previous adjusted position against physics and drawn geometry. It never changes the movement basis, attack, animation, FOV, or effects. Across 39 consecutive draw samples there were no blocks, and no added offset step exceeded 6.7 cm. The hit stayed on tick 165. The sword's projected base-to-tip span increased from 25.4 to 30.4 px on impact, with no shift of the core; other sampled swing poses did not grow. The camera was back at stock 19 ticks (317 ms) after impact.

In a separate replay an 18 px trusted drag on the right-side look surface after damage turned the native camera. The added framing stopped increasing on the first look tick (167), was gone eight ticks later (133 ms), and did not reengage when the touch ended. A unit test also clears the effect in first person; the replay did not switch perspective. This is a browser prototype, not a live Fable change, phone-play comfort verdict, or camera clearance guarantee outside this route.
