# Check the cover-to-sword route with the phone controls

Reuse the pinned Fable build, normal Rook, his light sword attack, and the staged low cover from the earlier keyboard comparison. The movement uses Chromium touch pointer input on Fable's floating stick, then releases it and taps the visible sword button. The test checks DOM hit targets and browser-trusted events. It does not inject keyboard movement, force sprint, teleport after setup, roll, or change collisions or combat. The test advances four native ticks between direction decisions; it does not claim a human player can repeat the timings exactly.

On lock, the early thumb path went around the crate and triggered the native `sword1` during Jack's existing recovery. The hit landed 0.57 s after the crash, about 0.23 s later than the separate keyboard run. The native touch sprint started only after sustained full input. Rook stayed at 204 HP. Holding off the route until 46 ticks into recovery left Rook short of a clear sword angle when Jack stood up. In the second run Jack still had 132 HP at recovery exit, no light hit landed, and his next jab took Rook from 204 to 185 HP before the route found a clear sword angle. That late replay shows a missed opening, not a failed sword-button tap: no sword tap was sent.

Run `node demos/mite-yard/jack-warning/charge-cover.mjs FABLE_CHECKOUT DEPENDENCY_PROJECT touch` against a locally served build of the pinned Fable checkout. The fixture writes numeric outcomes and private screenshots. This is a scripted test in a 402 × 874 Chrome viewport, not a physical iPhone playtest or a game update. No Fable content was copied into the public Catalog.
