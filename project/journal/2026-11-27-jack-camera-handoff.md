# Manual look moves the view rather than fighting it

In the previous live camera prototype, a small drag could counter-turn the picture because the presentation offset was removed in one frame. This iteration gives manual input priority immediately, but retires only the residual offset. During a drag, the old turn can decay by at most one quarter of the requested angular delta and 48° per second. With a stationary look pointer the horizontal offset holds. Widening unwinds at no more than 36° per second. Sprint intent and the brief release guard stop selecting a new automatic fit. Settings opt-out and switching camera mode still disable the effect outright.

In a 402 × 874 replay of the native counter, six calls to the native horizontal look-input method steered the displayed camera in the commanded direction, including small and larger inputs and a direction reversal. The first +0.60° input moved the picture +0.44°. At that same frame and preceding camera state, removing the residual turn instantly would have shifted the picture −10.11°. The baseline here is a same-frame immediate-clear projection, not a separate second combat playthrough. Some commanded movements were amplified by the stock camera and residual decay; this does not establish perfect one-to-one drag response.

The no-input run retained both rigs in all eight sampled poses (stock clipped Jack in five), and the reference charge, roll, damage and recovery still ran. Fifteen scripted frames of sprint-button intent stopped retargeting and allowed the residual to decay; the character was not in a legal sprint state during this combat action. The effect does not change movement-facing yaw, physical camera position or the gold Broken countdown. Reproduce with the same three v300 native probe modes and the updated `live-framing-proof.py`. No iPhone, wall or vertical-drag playtest; no Fable code or assets changed or published.
