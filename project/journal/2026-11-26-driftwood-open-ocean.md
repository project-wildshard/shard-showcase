# Driftwood becomes open ocean

The renovation left Driftwood as one big tropical island ringed by
islets. Jake wants the opposite feel: open ocean and cool islands of
every shape and size, like The Wind Waker and Black Flag, with Moana
and Sea of Thieves. So the intro, the whole-cube bullet and the
entrances changed: four edge islands carry the roads, as in Sky
Reach, instead of jetties. New: a mix of shallow and deep water, and
under the sea as half the zone (reefs, kelp, ruins, wrecks, caves,
trenches), with underwater exploration in Features.

I kept "never empty" for the open sea: Black Flag's sea is open but
always has something on it, and Jake's Sky Reach note was about wasted
space.

Then Jake went all out: as much under the sea as above it. The sea
floor is now a second world as big as the islands (coral cities,
drowned towns, an undersea village, a sunken temple dungeon), with
rideable sea creatures, ship combat like Black Flag, and treasure maps
on land or sea floor. "Swim forever and never drown" is gone: you
hold your breath for short dives until a quest wins you an underwater
breather, and dry air pockets, domed halls and caves under the sea
give you places to walk, breathe and fight.

Last, "go big or go home": an Opus agent redid In the zone from
scratch. Your own sailboat leads it (Jake: "GG"), then sea combat,
the undersea world, rideable sea creatures and treasure maps;
ziplines and cliff dives shrink to one optional line. Levels go to
20. Three mini dungeons now: the tidal wreck, a whirlpool you sail
your own boat down into, and a sunken palace in the deepest trench.
